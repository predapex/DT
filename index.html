Member / connection / footing checks
This is a conservative scenario screen, not a failure prediction or permission to climb. Each diagonal is checked separately as the sole vertical support; neither gets credit for the other. Side load is added to member demand, and overturning is enveloped over swing directions. These maxima are separate cases, not simultaneous reactions. Handrail is also checked for complete diagonal-support loss. Red means an unverified legacy screen is exceeded; purple means no capacity is established; grey means below an unverified screen, not safe. No green safety rating is used. The ⅓ deterioration loss is your assumed allowance, not a measured condition assessment. Unknown capacities remain unknown after reduction. See the point checks below.
Secondary tower load distribution
Purple arrows trace the secondary load paths after the gallows forces enter the tower at the lower knee-brace connection, boom/handrail connection, and upper angle/roof-frame connection. They show transfer into the existing framing, neighboring legs and foundations. Arrow thickness responds to the total load including both ropes. No beneficial load sharing is credited in the member checks. This is a load-path visualization, not a finite-element calculation of the exact force in each individual brace.
Rear footing uplift screen
Tower top is level. The gallows projects from the FRONT of the tower. Front tower legs are 3 ft longer than the rear legs over the 15 ft front-to-rear footing spacing. Red/orange arrows show the uplift-producing reaction at the actual REAR footing line, opposite the gallows. The displayed value is not net footing uplift: tower self-weight, footing/concrete weight, soil/rock restraint, guy-cable effects and anchor/rebar resistance are not subtracted because those values are not in the model. Swing direction is enveloped separately for each axis. Each rear footing is checked for 100% of the rear-line uplift component; a single-foot scenario also adds the transverse footing-line uplift component. No equal-split benefit is credited. These are separate load cases, not simultaneous footing reactions, and do not bound every possible frame-stiffness effect.

Screening values depend heavily on actual unsupported span, connection geometry, bolt grade/layout, welds, eccentricity, corrosion and anchorage. A structural engineer should replace these estimates with measured capacities before anyone climbs on it.
